# 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

## 1.1 Introduction

SudhaBelur is a website project for a boutique and tailoring business located in Belur, Hassan, Karnataka.

The website is intended to establish a professional digital presence for the business and allow customers to explore available clothing categories, inspect individual products, understand the services offered and request appointments online.

The administrator will manage the product catalogue and appointment requests through a protected administrative interface.

The project combines a customer-facing website with a backend management system. It is intended to be a practical business application rather than a demonstration website that only displays static information.

The website must support two primary activities:

1. **Customer discovery:** Customers browse products, review availability and prices where provided, and request appointments.
2. **Business administration:** The administrator maintains the catalogue and manually accepts or rejects appointment requests.

The website is designed around the existing business's service model. It is not intended to turn the business into a large online marketplace or automated retail operation.

# 2. ORIGIN AND EVOLUTION OF THE PROJECT

This section records how the project's requirements evolved, including ideas that were subsequently narrowed or clarified.

## 2.1 Initial concept

The original concept was to build a professional website for a clothing, tailoring and embroidery business.

The initial vision included a premium homepage, a brand story, a service catalogue, a product catalogue, inquiry forms and an administrative dashboard.

The website was intended to make the business discoverable online and give potential customers a clearer understanding of its offerings.

The initial concept was broader than the final MVP.

## 2.2 Establishing the brand

The project was initially discussed under the internal codename Juliet.

The actual business name was subsequently established as SudhaBelur.

The business is based in Belur, Hassan, Karnataka.

From this point onward, SudhaBelur became the primary name for the website, brand presentation and customer-facing experience.

## 2.3 Clarifying the business model

One of the most important decisions was clarifying that SudhaBelur is not a conventional e-commerce business.

The business does not intend to sell ready-made clothing through an automated online purchasing process.

Instead, customers should be able to browse the catalogue and request an appointment related to a particular product or clothing category.

The administrator then reviews the request and manually approves or rejects it.

This decision changes the entire application design.

A traditional e-commerce application normally requires a cart, checkout, payment processing, order fulfilment and shipping workflows. SudhaBelur does not require these systems for its initial release.

The product catalogue is therefore primarily a discovery and appointment-request interface.

## 2.4 Simplifying the MVP

The project was deliberately reduced to a manageable set of features.

The following became the core MVP:

- Premium business homepage.
- Product catalogue.
- Product categories.
- Product images and descriptions.
- Optional product prices.
- Product availability indicators.
- Appointment request workflow.
- Contact information.
- Frequently asked questions.
- Administrator authentication.
- Product management.
- Appointment approval and rejection.
- PostgreSQL persistence.

The project was not intended to begin with distributed infrastructure or a complex enterprise architecture.

Features such as Redis, Kafka, Elasticsearch, Kubernetes, microservices and extensive deployment automation were explicitly excluded from the initial scope.

The intention was to build a functional, maintainable product first.

## 2.5 Establishing the technology stack

The selected stack became:

- Next.js for the frontend.
- Java Spring Boot for the backend.
- PostgreSQL for persistent storage.

The frontend and backend are separate application layers.

The frontend is responsible for rendering the customer experience and administrative interface. The backend is responsible for business rules, authentication, data access and appointment management.

PostgreSQL stores the application's persistent business data.

## 2.6 Moving from a template to a custom design

A premium luxury e-commerce template from v0 was used as an initial reference.

Reference template:

https://v0.app/templates/premium-luxury-e-commerce-template-Umx1e3VQmpL?b=b_BjlUTr5oMC4

The template was not intended to define the final website.

The intended direction was to use it as a starting point for understanding layout and structure, then redesign the actual interface around SudhaBelur's brand, service model and visual preferences.

The imported template and its dependencies were explored, and the Next.js development environment was brought up successfully after installing the required dependencies.

The frontend was subsequently intended to be customized rather than accepted as a finished product.

## 2.7 Building the backend from the ground up

The backend development progressed through database setup and the initial product domain model.

PostgreSQL was installed locally. The `sudhabelur` database was established, and Spring Boot was connected to it.

The `Product` entity and `Category` enum were created, Hibernate successfully created the product table, and `ProductRepository` was completed.

The next recorded backend development step was to create the service layer, beginning with product-related business logic.

This is the last confirmed implementation checkpoint recorded in the project history available for this document. Later progress must be verified before being considered complete.

- Kurti-related services.
- Lehenga-related services.

The exact operational details of each service, such as accepted fabrics, turnaround times, measurement procedures and pricing rules, must reflect the real business and should not be invented by the application.

## 3.2 Digital presence

The website will serve as the business's online point of discovery.

A visitor should be able to determine:

- What SudhaBelur offers.
- Which product categories are available.
- Whether an individual catalogue item is currently available.
- What price has been specified, if any.
- How to request an appointment.
- How to contact the business.
- What to expect from the appointment process.

The website must communicate these details without creating the false impression that customers can complete an online purchase.

## 3.3 Intended business benefits

The project is expected to provide the following benefits:

1. Establish a dedicated online presence.
2. Present products and services in a consistent format.
3. Make catalogue updates easier for the administrator.
4. Reduce the need to communicate every basic product detail individually.
5. Organize appointment requests in a central location.
6. Allow the administrator to review requests before confirming appointments.
7. Improve the consistency of customer-facing information.
8. Provide a foundation for future improvements without requiring a complete rebuild.

These are intended benefits, not measured business outcomes. Actual improvements would need to be evaluated after the website becomes operational.

# 4. BUSINESS MODEL AND OPERATING PRINCIPLES

## 4.1 Service-oriented model

SudhaBelur follows a service-oriented model rather than a conventional online retail model.

The catalogue helps customers discover products and services. The appointment workflow provides a way to express interest and initiate contact.

An appointment request is not automatically a confirmed appointment.

The administrator must explicitly approve or reject the request.

## 4.2 Customer interaction model

The intended flow is:

1. A customer visits the website.
2. The customer browses the catalogue.
3. The customer opens a product's details.
4. The customer chooses to request an appointment.
5. The customer provides the required information.
6. The application records the request.
7. The administrator reviews the request.
8. The administrator approves or rejects it.
9. The resulting status is made available through the application's supported customer communication workflow.

The exact method used to notify customers of a decision must be established before that functionality is considered complete.

## 4.3 Product catalogue versus inventory

The catalogue is not intended to represent a sophisticated warehouse inventory system.

Each product has a simple availability indicator.

An unavailable product must remain distinguishable from an available product. The catalogue should show the unavailable status clearly rather than allowing customers to assume the product is available.

The current database design uses a Boolean availability field. This supports a simple available/unavailable distinction.

It does not, by itself, represent quantities, stock movements, reservations or inventory history.

## 4.4 Appointment requests versus orders

An appointment request is not an order.

The application must not imply that:

- A payment has been made.
- A product has been purchased.
- A delivery has been scheduled.
- A tailoring job has been accepted.
- A particular completion date has been guaranteed.

Those actions are outside the current MVP unless the business explicitly changes its requirements.

## 4.5 Manual business decisions

The administrator retains control over appointment acceptance.

The website assists the business by collecting and organizing requests. It does not replace the administrator's judgment or independently promise a booking.

# 5. PROBLEM STATEMENT

A local tailoring and boutique business can benefit from a structured online presence, but a generic retail website may not match its actual operations.

A static website can display information, but it cannot provide a centralized way to manage product availability and appointment requests.

A conventional e-commerce system may introduce unnecessary complexity when the business does not require automated checkout or online payment.

SudhaBelur therefore requires a lightweight full-stack application that combines:

- A polished public website.
- A manageable product catalogue.
- An appointment-request workflow.
- A protected administrative interface.
- Persistent storage.
- A clear distinction between requested and confirmed appointments.

The challenge is to provide a high-quality customer experience without building unnecessary infrastructure.

# 9. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

The following requirements describe the intended system behavior. They are specifications, not proof that the functionality has already been implemented.

## 9.1 Homepage

The homepage must introduce SudhaBelur and provide clear navigation to the main areas of the website.

It should communicate the business's identity, showcase selected imagery and direct visitors toward the catalogue or appointment process.

The homepage must not rely on fake customer testimonials or fabricated claims about the business.

## 9.2 Product catalogue

The catalogue must present products in an organized, browsable format.

Each product may contain:

- Product name.
- Category.
- Description.
- Price.
- Availability.
- One or more images.

A product without an entered price must not display an invented price or an incorrect zero price.

An unavailable product must display the word **Unavailable** in red.

The catalogue must reflect the current database state rather than rely on hardcoded product data in the production application.

## 9.3 Category navigation

The initial supported categories are:

1. BLOUSE
2. KURTI
3. LEHENGA
4. GOWN
5. SAREE
6. SALWAR_SUIT
7. WOMEN_WEAR

These categories correspond to the existing backend category model and database constraint.

The customer-facing interface may use more readable labels, such as "Salwar Suit" instead of `SALWAR_SUIT`.

The initial scope does not include deleting categories.

## 9.4 Product details

Opening a product should reveal enough information for the customer to understand the item and decide whether to request an appointment.

Product details should include the product's name, category, description, images, availability and price when specified.

The appointment action should be clearly associated with the selected product.

## 9.5 Appointment request

The application must provide a way for customers to request an appointment.

The confirmed core requirement is that the request includes the customer's name, phone number and selected product or service.

The exact set of additional fields, such as preferred date, preferred time, comments or alternate contact details, remains to be finalized.

Submitting a request must create a persisted record and return a clear result to the customer.

The application must not report success if the backend has failed to save the request.

## 9.6 Product administration

The administrator must be able to create, edit and delete catalogue products.

The product form must support the fields defined by the product model.

The administrator must be able to change product availability.

The application must validate required fields and reject invalid category values.

## 9.7 Appointment administration

The administrator must be able to view incoming requests and inspect their details.

The administrator must be able to approve or reject a request.

The application must persist the decision and the resulting appointment status.

The exact policy for modifying a previously approved or rejected request remains an open business rule.

## 9.8 Contact page

The Contact Us page must display the business's approved contact information.

The exact phone number, email address, opening hours and map information must come from verified business details.

These details must not be invented during development.

## 9.9 FAQ page

The FAQ page must answer common questions about the business and its appointment process.

The content should be consistent with the actual services offered.

Questions about payment, delivery, appointment confirmation, pricing and turnaround times must be answered only after the business's policies have been confirmed.

# 10. CUSTOMER JOURNEY AND WORKFLOWS

## 10.1 Customer discovery workflow

1. The customer opens the website.
2. The homepage introduces the brand.
3. The customer selects Shop.
4. The customer browses the available categories.
5. The customer opens a product.
6. The customer checks the product's details and availability.
7. The customer decides whether to request an appointment.

The process should require as few unnecessary steps as possible.

## 10.2 Appointment request workflow

1. The customer selects the appointment action for a product.
2. The application opens an appointment form or dialog.
3. The selected product is identified in the form.
4. The customer provides their name and phone number.
5. The customer supplies any other required information.
6. The frontend validates basic input.
7. The backend validates the request again.
8. The backend stores the appointment request.
9. The application displays a submission confirmation.
10. The administrator reviews the request.

The request must not be treated as confirmed merely because it was submitted.

## 10.3 Appointment state model

The initial workflow requires at least three states:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pending | The request has been submitted and awaits a decision |
| Approved | The administrator has accepted the request |
| Rejected | The administrator has declined the request |

A more advanced status model is not required for the MVP.

The application must distinguish the current status from the historical fact that a request was submitted.

## 10.4 Product unavailable workflow

When a product is unavailable:

- The catalogue displays its unavailable status in red.
- The application does not represent it as currently available.
- The customer is not misled about its status.
- The business may still display the product for informational purposes.

Whether appointment requests should be disabled for unavailable products is a separate business rule that must be confirmed. A frontend-only restriction would not be sufficient; the backend must enforce the final policy.

## 10.5 Error workflow

If an appointment cannot be saved:

- The customer must receive a clear error message.
- The application must not show a false success confirmation.
- The entered information should not be discarded unnecessarily.
- The backend should record sufficient diagnostic information without logging sensitive customer details indiscriminately.

# 11. ADMINISTRATOR WORKFLOW

## 11.1 Sign-in

The administrator opens the protected administrative area and authenticates.

The backend verifies the credentials and any additional authentication requirements.

Only authorized access should allow entry into the administrative workspace.

## 11.2 Product management

The administrator can create a new product by entering the required product information.

The administrator can subsequently update the name, category, description, price, availability and images.

The administrator can remove a product when it should no longer appear in the catalogue.

Changes must be persisted to PostgreSQL and reflected in the public website.

## 11.3 Appointment management

The administrator opens the appointment management area.

Each request should display enough information to identify the request, including the customer, associated product or service, submission time and current status.

The administrator reviews the request and chooses approval or rejection.

The system updates the status and makes the decision available to the relevant application workflows.

## 11.4 Product deletion and related appointments

An earlier requirement specified that deleting a product should delete its associated pending and rejected appointment records.

This requirement needs particular care.

A broad cascade-delete rule can unintentionally erase business records, especially when an appointment has already been approved.

The original deletion requirement should therefore be treated as a policy requiring confirmation before implementation. It must not be applied indiscriminately to every appointment state.

A safer design would preserve historical records and prevent deletion when business obligations remain. If the original deletion behavior is retained, it must be narrowly defined, explicitly confirmed and tested.

## 11.5 Administrative usability

The administrative interface should prioritize practical work rather than decorative complexity.

The administrator should be able to identify pending requests quickly, inspect the relevant details and complete routine product updates without navigating through unrelated sections.

# 12. PRODUCT CATALOGUE AND CATEGORY MANAGEMENT

## 12.1 Product data model

The current database product table contains these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `id` | Unique product identifier |
| `name` | Product name |
| `category` | Product category |
| `description` | Product description |
| `price` | Optional product price |
| `available` | Availability flag |
| `created_at` | Creation timestamp |
| `updated_at` | Last-update timestamp |

The exact nullability rules, timestamp behavior and validation requirements should be verified against the current database schema and entity definitions.

## 12.2 Product identifiers

The product table uses a `bigint` identity column for its primary key.

The identifier is generated by the database rather than manually selected by the administrator.

## 12.3 Price handling

The original requirement allows product prices to be left blank when no price has been entered.

The application must distinguish a missing price from a price of zero.

A zero price is a numeric value. A missing price means the business has not specified a value for that product.

The frontend should therefore render a price only when an actual price exists.

The current database uses a numeric price field. Whether it permits null values must be checked before relying on this behavior.

## 12.4 Availability

The product table uses a Boolean `available` field.

This is appropriate for the initial two-state availability requirement.

The customer interface must translate the stored value into understandable language.

- Available products should be presented as available.
- Unavailable products should show **Unavailable** in red.

The visual distinction must remain understandable without relying on color alone.

## 12.5 Product images

Each product may have one or more images.

The current recorded product table does not contain an image field.

Therefore, image storage and retrieval remain a necessary design and implementation task.

The final approach must support multiple images per product without forcing image binaries into the main product record.

The storage mechanism, image metadata, upload restrictions and image deletion policy have not yet been confirmed.

## 12.6 Category validation

The database has a category check constraint that restricts values to the seven supported categories.

This protects the database against unsupported category values.

Validation should also occur in the backend before persistence so that customers and administrators receive meaningful errors rather than database constraint failures.

The initial scope does not include deleting categories.

# 13. APPOINTMENT MANAGEMENT

## 13.1 Purpose

The appointment subsystem is responsible for collecting customer requests and allowing the administrator to decide how to handle them.

It is one of the two primary functional components of the MVP, alongside product management.

## 13.2 Minimum appointment information

The confirmed minimum information includes:

- Customer name.
- Customer phone number.
- Associated product or service.
- Request status.

The application will also need a unique appointment identifier and a submission timestamp for reliable record management.

These are design requirements for the appointment subsystem; the appointment table's current implementation status has not been confirmed.

## 13.3 Additional fields requiring a decision

Potential fields include:

- Preferred appointment date.
- Preferred appointment time.
- Customer comments.
- Creation timestamp.
- Decision timestamp.
- Reason for rejection.

Only fields justified by the real appointment process should be implemented.

In particular, a preferred date and time are useful only if the business intends customers to specify them when submitting a request.

## 13.4 Appointment status rules

New requests should begin in the pending state.

Only the administrator should be able to approve or reject them.

Status changes must be persisted and validated by the backend.

The system must not silently convert a pending request into an approved appointment.

## 13.5 Concurrency and duplicate submissions

The backend should account for repeated submissions, including accidental double-clicks and network retries.

A repeated request must not silently create multiple appointments when the application can reasonably prevent the duplication.

The exact duplicate-detection policy must be designed around the information collected. Two requests from the same phone number may represent legitimate separate appointments, so the phone number alone should not be used as a universal uniqueness key.

## 13.6 Customer notification

The system needs a clear way for customers to understand whether their request was submitted and whether the administrator has made a decision.

The initial requirement establishes manual administrator approval or rejection, but it does not establish an implemented SMS, email or messaging integration.

A submission confirmation can be provided directly on the website. Notifications about later status changes require a separately selected delivery mechanism.

# 18. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

## 18.1 Architectural overview

The intended architecture consists of three primary layers.

### Layer 1: Presentation

The Next.js application presents the customer website and the protected administrative interface.

It collects input and displays results from the backend.

### Layer 2: Application and business logic

The Spring Boot application validates requests, applies business rules, enforces authorization and coordinates persistence.

### Layer 3: Data persistence

PostgreSQL stores products and, once implemented, appointment records and other required business data.

## 18.2 Logical request flow

A typical product catalogue request follows this sequence:

1. The browser requests the Shop page.
2. The Next.js application requests product data from the backend.
3. The backend receives the API request.
4. The application service performs the required operation.
5. The repository retrieves data from PostgreSQL.
6. The backend returns the result.
7. The frontend renders the product catalogue.

An appointment request follows a similar path, except that the backend validates the submitted information and creates a new record.

An administrative action must additionally pass authentication and authorization checks.

## 18.3 Architectural boundaries

The frontend is responsible for presentation, not authoritative business enforcement.

The controller is responsible for handling the HTTP request and response, not for containing the entire business logic.

The service layer is responsible for application rules and orchestration.

The repository layer is responsible for database access.

The database is responsible for enforcing its defined integrity constraints.

These boundaries help keep the project understandable and reduce accidental coupling.

## 18.4 Architecture diagram

Browser  
↓  
Next.js frontend  
↓  
Spring Boot REST API  
↓  
Service layer  
↓  
Repository layer  
↓  
PostgreSQL database

Administrative requests pass through the same backend boundary but require appropriate authorization before protected operations are executed.

## 18.5 Deployment boundary

During local development, the frontend, backend and database may run as separate local processes.

In deployment, the frontend and backend may use different hosting services, with PostgreSQL hosted on a managed database service or another suitable environment.

The exact production providers and connection arrangements have not yet been finalized.

# 20. DATABASE DESIGN

## 20.1 Database identification

The development database is named `sudhabelur`.

PostgreSQL was installed locally, and the application was connected to the database during development.

The recorded local PostgreSQL server used port `5432`.

The database was inspected through `psql`, and the product table was confirmed to exist.

## 20.2 Existing products table

The recorded table is `public.products`.

Its columns are:

| Column | Type | Recorded purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `bigint` | Primary key, identity-generated |
| `available` | `boolean` | Product availability |
| `category` | `varchar(255)` | Product category |
| `created_at` | `timestamp` | Record creation time |
| `description` | `varchar(255)` | Product description |
| `name` | `varchar(255)` | Product name |
| `price` | `numeric(38,2)` | Product price |
| `updated_at` | `timestamp` | Last-update time |

This is the recorded schema at the time of the latest confirmed checkpoint. The live schema may have changed since then.

## 20.3 Primary key

The product table has a primary-key constraint named `products_pkey`.

It uses a B-tree index on the `id` column.

The identifier is generated by the database.

## 20.4 Category constraint

The table contains a category check constraint named `products_category_check`.

The constraint restricts category values to:

- BLOUSE
- KURTI
- LEHENGA
- GOWN
- SAREE
- SALWAR_SUIT
- WOMEN_WEAR

The constraint prevents unsupported category values from being stored in the product table.

## 20.5 Price representation

The database uses a numeric field with two decimal places for the product price.

This is appropriate for monetary values.

However, the application must separately establish whether the field allows null values, whether negative prices are rejected and how values are formatted for display.

The current requirement is to leave the displayed price blank when no price has been specified.

## 20.6 Timestamp handling

The product table contains `created_at` and `updated_at`.

The application should establish a consistent policy for setting and updating these values.

The actual timezone strategy and automatic-update behavior should be verified rather than assumed.

## 20.7 Appointment table

An appointment table is required for the intended application, but its implementation has not been confirmed in the recorded development checkpoint.

The table should store the appointment identifier, customer details, associated product or service, request status and required timestamps.

The final schema should be designed after the business rules and product-deletion policy are settled.

## 20.8 Product image storage

The current products table does not contain a recorded image field.

A separate image metadata model or equivalent structure is therefore needed if products support multiple images.

The image data itself may be stored in a suitable object-storage service while PostgreSQL stores the relevant metadata and references.

The final choice has not been established.

## 20.9 Relational integrity

Appointment records associated with products require an explicit deletion policy.

Blindly cascading every product deletion into appointment deletion can destroy useful historical records.

The database design should protect the integrity of existing appointments and reflect the business's approved retention rules.

## 20.10 Database schema management

Hibernate created the product table during development.

The project should establish a controlled schema-change strategy before production deployment.

Automatic schema creation is useful during initial development, but production database changes should be deliberate and reviewable.

# 22. AUTHENTICATION AND SECURITY

## 22.1 Security objective

The administrative interface controls product information and appointment decisions.

Unauthorized users must not be able to access these capabilities merely by discovering an endpoint or navigating directly to an administrative URL.

## 22.2 Single administrator

The current business requirement is for one administrator.

The MVP does not require a general multi-user administrative platform.

The security model should be designed around one authorized administrative identity while avoiding unnecessary complexity.

## 22.3 Authentication

The application needs a secure way to verify the administrator's identity.

The exact authentication mechanism and credential-provisioning process must be finalized.

If passwords are used, they must be stored using an appropriate password-hashing mechanism. Plaintext passwords must never be stored.

## 22.4 Two-factor authentication

Strong administrative security, including two-factor authentication, was explicitly requested.

SMS-based one-time passwords were discussed as a possible method.

However, the actual 2FA implementation and SMS provider have not been confirmed.

The system should not be described as having 2FA until the feature is implemented and tested.

A suitable 2FA design must address OTP expiry, single-use behavior, attempt limits, replay prevention and recovery procedures.

## 22.5 Authorization

Administrative authorization must be enforced by the backend.

Every protected operation must verify the authenticated identity and required permissions.

A frontend route guard is useful for user experience but is not a replacement for backend authorization.

## 22.6 Session and token security

The final authentication mechanism must define how authentication state is maintained.

If browser cookies are used, the application should configure suitable security attributes and address cross-site request risks where applicable.

If bearer tokens are used, token storage, expiry, revocation and refresh behavior must be designed deliberately.

These decisions should be based on the selected implementation rather than copied from an unrelated tutorial.

## 22.7 Input validation

All user input must be validated on the backend.

Examples include:

- Customer name.
- Phone number.
- Product name.
- Product description.
- Category.
- Price.
- Appointment identifier.
- Appointment status.

Validation should include reasonable length limits and appropriate data types.

## 22.8 Abuse prevention

The appointment endpoint is publicly accessible by design, so it may attract automated or repeated submissions.

The initial implementation should at least consider reasonable request limits, validation, duplicate-submission handling and monitoring of unusual activity.

These protections should be proportionate to the small MVP rather than introducing a large infrastructure dependency.

## 22.9 Secrets and database access

Database credentials, application secrets and authentication keys must not be exposed in frontend bundles or public repositories.

The production database should not be directly accessible from the public internet unless the hosting architecture explicitly requires it and access is restricted appropriately.

## 22.10 Logging and privacy

Logs should help diagnose application failures without unnecessarily recording customer phone numbers, passwords, OTPs or authentication secrets.

Administrative actions should produce enough information to diagnose unexpected changes, while keeping the logging design proportionate to the MVP.

# 29. TESTING STRATEGY

## 29.1 Unit testing

Unit tests should cover isolated business rules.

Examples include:

- Product validation.
- Category validation.
- Price validation.
- Appointment initial status.
- Appointment status transitions.
- Rejection of invalid operations.

## 29.2 Repository and database testing

Database-related tests should verify that:

- Products can be saved and retrieved.
- Category constraints behave as expected.
- Product updates persist.
- Appointment records persist.
- Relationships remain consistent.
- Invalid database operations fail safely.

## 29.3 API testing

API tests should cover:

- Valid product requests.
- Invalid product requests.
- Missing product identifiers.
- Valid appointment submissions.
- Invalid appointment submissions.
- Approval and rejection.
- Unauthorized administrative requests.
- Unexpected server errors.

Postman or automated integration tests may be used as appropriate.

## 29.4 Frontend testing

Frontend tests should verify:

- Navigation.
- Catalogue rendering.
- Product details.
- Optional price rendering.
- Availability labels.
- Appointment form validation.
- Submission feedback.
- Administrative form behavior.
- Loading and error states.
- Responsive layouts.

## 29.5 End-to-end testing

The most important test is the complete business workflow.

A customer submits an appointment request, the backend saves it, the administrator sees it, the administrator approves or rejects it, and the resulting status is persisted correctly.

Testing isolated screens without verifying this complete sequence is insufficient.

## 29.6 Security testing

Security testing should verify that:

- Public users cannot create or delete products.
- Public users cannot approve appointments.
- Unauthenticated users cannot access protected management data.
- Invalid authentication is rejected.
- Expired authentication cannot be reused.
- Input validation cannot be bypassed by directly calling the API.
- Secrets are not exposed in public responses or frontend assets.

The catalogue should load efficiently for a small business website.

Product and appointment data must be persisted reliably.

## 30.8 Compatibility

# 31. RISKS AND MITIGATION

## 31.1 Scope expansion

**Risk:** Adding payments, delivery, customer accounts and advanced infrastructure before the core appointment workflow works.

**Mitigation:** Treat the MVP scope as a boundary. New features require explicit decisions about business value, cost and implementation effort.

## 31.2 Premature infrastructure complexity

**Risk:** Introducing Redis, Kafka, Elasticsearch or microservices without a real need.

**Mitigation:** Use the existing Next.js, Spring Boot and PostgreSQL architecture until actual requirements justify additional components.

## 31.3 Weak administrative security

**Risk:** Protecting admin pages only in the frontend.

**Mitigation:** Enforce authentication and authorization in the backend, then verify them through direct API tests.

## 31.4 Incorrect appointment semantics

**Risk:** Treating submitted requests as confirmed bookings.

**Mitigation:** Maintain distinct pending, approved and rejected states.

## 31.5 Accidental data loss

**Risk:** Deleting appointment records when a product is removed.

**Mitigation:** Finalize and test the retention policy before enabling product deletion.

## 31.6 Image-storage complexity

**Risk:** Implementing product imagery without a clear storage strategy.

**Mitigation:** Decide how images are uploaded, stored, referenced and removed before completing image management.

## 31.7 Free-tier limitations

**Risk:** Depending on hosting that sleeps, imposes limits or changes its terms.

**Mitigation:** Validate current provider restrictions and have a realistic plan for production persistence and availability.

## 31.8 Incomplete customer communication

**Risk:** Customers submit requests but have no way to understand the decision later.

**Mitigation:** Define the post-submission communication process before launch. Do not assume SMS or email notifications exist until implemented.

## 31.9 Documentation drift

**Risk:** The implementation changes while the project documentation remains outdated.

**Mitigation:** Update the status table and relevant design decisions as development progresses.

# 36. DEFINITION OF DONE

A task is not complete merely because code has been written or a page appears in the browser.

A feature is complete when:

1. Its business requirement is clear.
2. Its backend behavior is implemented where required.
3. Its data model is correct.
4. Its validation rules are enforced.
5. Its frontend is connected to the real backend.
6. Expected success cases work.
7. Expected failure cases work.
8. Unauthorized access is rejected where applicable.
9. Data persists correctly.
10. The feature has been tested.
11. The documentation reflects its actual status.

For example, the product management feature is not complete just because an administrator can fill out a product form.

The feature is complete when the product is validated, saved to PostgreSQL, returned through the API, displayed in the catalogue and protected against unauthorized modification.

The same principle applies to appointment management.

A visually complete appointment form is not a complete appointment workflow unless the request is persisted, the administrator can process it and the status is handled correctly.

# 37. FINAL PROJECT SUMMARY

SudhaBelur is a full-stack website project intended to support a boutique, tailoring and embroidery business in Belur, Hassan, Karnataka.

The project evolved from a broader website concept into a focused appointment-based service application.

Its core purpose is to present the business professionally, display a manageable product catalogue and allow customers to request appointments.

The administrator retains control over product information and appointment decisions.

The selected technology stack is Next.js, Java Spring Boot and PostgreSQL.

The frontend provides the public website and administrative interface. The backend implements business rules, data access and protected operations. PostgreSQL stores persistent business data.

The project intentionally avoids unnecessary e-commerce functionality and infrastructure during the MVP stage.

The recorded implementation has progressed through PostgreSQL installation, creation of the `sudhabelur` database, Spring Boot database connectivity, the `Product` entity, the `Category` enum, creation of the `products` table and completion of `ProductRepository`.

The next recorded backend milestone is the product service layer, followed by the remaining product API, appointment subsystem, security, frontend integration, testing and deployment.

The complete project must be judged by its working business workflows, not by the number of technologies used or the visual sophistication of individual pages.

**The central objective is straightforward: build a secure, polished, maintainable and cost-conscious application that allows customers to discover SudhaBelur's services and request appointments, while allowing the administrator to manage the catalogue and process those requests reliably.**
